# Paper Card — hart2006_nasa_tlx_20_years_later

## Full citation (APA)
- Hart, S. G. (2006). NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX); 20 Years Later. *Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting, 50*(9), 904–908.

## Link / DOI
- https://doi.org/10.1177/154193120605000909

## Problem (1–3 sentences)
- J3 must quantify cognitive workload differences across autonomy conditions.
- A multidimensional workload instrument helps diagnose *why* workload changes.

## Approach (what they did)
- “20 years later” overview of NASA-TLX usage and validation, including implementation variants.

## Measures (what they measured)
- NASA-TLX subscales: Mental Demand, Physical Demand, Temporal Demand, Performance, Effort, Frustration.
- Composite scoring:
  - Weighted TLX (pairwise comparisons)
  - Raw TLX (RTLX; simple average; widely used for practicality)

## Key results / takeaways
- RTLX is widely adopted in practice due to logistics and can be sensitive enough for many comparisons (as summarized in the review).

## Limitations / failure modes
- Subjective measure; can be influenced by success/failure.
- “Performance” subscale needs careful instructions.

## What we reuse (for J3)
- NASA-TLX as primary subjective workload instrument.
- Use subscales diagnostically (e.g., high success + high frustration → agency boundary issue).
- Prefer RTLX for repeated blocks unless weighting is essential.

## What we must NOT repeat (avoid redundancy vs C3/C1/C2)
- Don’t claim cognitive-state adaptation based on TLX alone; pair with pupil workload proxy and (optionally) objective workload task.
