# Paper Card — alonso2018_transparency_shared_autonomy

## Full citation (APA)
- Alonso, V., & de la Puente, P. (2018). System Transparency in Shared Autonomy: A Mini Review. *Frontiers in Neurorobotics, 12*, 83.

## Link / DOI
- https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbot.2018.00083

## Problem (1–3 sentences)
- Shared autonomy can fail in practice if users cannot predict or understand why the system intervenes.
- In prosthetics, surprises can be interpreted as loss of control, harming trust and perceived agency.

## Approach (what they did)
- Mini-review of transparency in shared autonomy: definitions, dimensions (observability/predictability), and implications for trust calibration.

## Key takeaways (J3-relevant)
- Transparency supports predictability and appropriate reliance.
- Boundary/authority cues can reduce surprise autonomy.

## Limitations / failure modes
- Review-level; J3 still needs to operationalize transparency/trust measures carefully.
- Too much transparency can increase workload.

## What we reuse (for J3)
- Justification for including trust/transparency considerations with agency and workload.
- Design constraint: interventions should be legible/predictable.

## What we must NOT repeat (avoid redundancy vs C3/C1/C2)
- Avoid heavy UI that increases mental demand; measure TLX frustration/mental demand.
